# Morning Tee Style Guide

## Voice

Morning Tee should sound smart, clean, approachable, and lightly witty. The ideal section feels like a sharp golf friend explaining what matters without pretending the story is bigger than it is.

Use:
- Natural contractions.
- Short setup, specific detail, clear takeaway.
- Plain golf language casual players understand.
- One memorable idea per section.
- Light wit when it clarifies or makes the read easier.

Avoid:
- Stiff sportswriting.
- Empty hype.
- Overstuffed intros.
- Forced jokes.
- Vague praise like "elite competitor" unless the next line proves it.

## Sentence Style

Prefer:
- "The interesting part is..."
- "That matters because..."
- "For regular golfers, the lesson is..."
- "The small detail worth watching..."

Avoid:
- "Only time will tell."
- "He will look to build momentum."
- "In a stunning turn of events."
- "Golf fans were treated to..."
- "The stage is set."
- "Poised to make noise."

## Humor Boundaries

Good humor is small and useful. It should make the point easier to remember.

Good: "That is not a swing thought. That is a tax form."

Bad: "This guy choked so hard he needs a Heimlich."

Never punch down, mock amateurs, or make injury/rules situations cute.

## Good vs Bad Phrasing

Bad: "Scottie Scheffler continues to dominate the golf world and will look to keep his momentum going."

Good: "Scheffler's edge is not that he has one magic club. It is that he almost never gives a shot away twice."

Bad: "This course demands accuracy and precision."

Good: "This course punishes the second miss. One bad drive is survivable. The recovery shot into the wrong side of the green is where rounds leak away."

## Before/After Rewrites

Before: "Rory is hoping to bounce back after a difficult finish."

After: "The question is not whether Rory can make birdies. It is whether he can turn one loose swing into bogey instead of a three-hole wobble."

Before: "The rules situation was confusing for fans."

After: "The ruling looked strange because golf separates intent, lie, and procedure. Once you know which bucket the rule lives in, the decision makes more sense."
