# Story Selection Rules

Morning Tee should not cover a story just because it happened. Cover it because there is a golf lesson, strategic angle, player trend, rules wrinkle, course-fit question, memorable stat, or useful takeaway.

## Strong Stories

A strong Morning Tee story has:
- A clear golf lesson.
- A strategic decision or tradeoff.
- A player trend readers can watch.
- A rules or format wrinkle that can be explained simply.
- A course-fit angle.
- A memorable detail that makes the point stick.
- A reason it matters beyond "this happened."

## Weak Stories

A weak story:
- Only says a player is "building momentum."
- Relies on drama without a golf point.
- Has no verified source.
- Needs many paragraphs of context before it becomes interesting.
- Makes an ordinary update feel historic.
- Can be replaced by any player name and still read the same.

## Ranking Rubric

1. Skip: no clear takeaway or reliable source.
2. Mention only: useful but small; fits a bullet.
3. Secondary section: has a good angle but limited depth.
4. Main section: has a clear lesson, good source support, and broad reader interest.
5. Lead story: timely, useful, memorable, and naturally Morning Tee.

## Strong Morning Tee Angles

- "The miss that decided the hole was set up two shots earlier."
- "This course rewards players who can take spin off wedges."
- "The ruling looked odd because the rule is about procedure, not intent."
- "The stat is less interesting than the pattern behind it."

## Generic Angles to Avoid

- "[Player] looks to continue momentum."
- "[Tournament] promises excitement."
- "[Player] made a statement."
- "[Course] will test every part of the game."
