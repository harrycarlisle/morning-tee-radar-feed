# Golf Analysis Rules

## Player Analysis

Look for:
- Where the player gains or loses shots.
- Whether the weakness is technical, strategic, or situational.
- Patterns under pressure.
- Shot shape, approach distance, wedge control, scrambling, putting volatility.
- Whether the current course amplifies or hides the weakness.

Do not turn every player into a hero arc. Make the golf point.

## Course Analysis

Explain:
- What the course asks players to do.
- Which misses are punished.
- Which approach distances matter.
- Whether greens reward height, spin, patience, or angles.
- How weather changes the course.

Useful frame: "What skill does this course ask for more than usual?"

## Strategy

Show the decision, not just the result.

Cover:
- Aggressive vs conservative target.
- Layup vs carry.
- When bogey avoidance beats birdie hunting.
- Why a player accepted a harder putt to avoid a worse miss.

## Regular-Golfer Takeaway

Every main section should answer: "What can a normal golfer learn?"

Examples:
- Aim away from the short-side miss.
- Match club choice to your bad shot, not your best shot.
- A conservative target with a committed swing beats a brave target with doubt.

## Rules And Rulings

Use a calm sequence:
1. What happened.
2. Which rule bucket applies.
3. What options the player had.
4. Why the outcome followed.
5. What golfers should remember.

Never invent rules details. Flag uncertainty.

## Historical Comparisons

Use history to clarify, not inflate.

Good: "This resembles [past example] because both depended on wedge control."

Bad: "This proves we are witnessing history."

Verify dates, wins, majors, and records before publishing.
