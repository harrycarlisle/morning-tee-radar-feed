# The Easiest Way to Lower Your Score Is Not Hitting It Farther

**Suggested Beehiiv slug:** `stories-lower-your-score-smarter-targets`  
**Kicker:** Strategy  
**Author:** Morning Tee  
**Suggested hero image alt:** Golfer studying a green before choosing a target line.

---

Most golfers think lower scores start with better ball-striking.

Hit it farther. Hit it straighter. Fix the path. Fix the face. Add speed.

Those things matter. But for the average golfer, they are usually the slowest path to better scoring.

The faster path is simpler: pick better targets.

## Scoring rewards decisions more than most golfers realize

Tour players are great ball-strikers, but what separates good rounds from bad ones is often decision-making.

Where they aim.  
What they avoid.  
What kind of miss they accept.

That is why strokes gained has become such a useful idea. It does not just ask whether a shot was hit well. It asks whether the shot helped scoring.

A solid 7-iron to the middle of the green often beats a aggressive line at the pin that brings short-sided trouble into play.

For most golfers, the scoring mistake is not always poor contact. It is choosing a target that turns one small miss into a big number.

## The middle of the green is underrated

Amateurs often aim at pins because flags are visible and satisfying.

But flags move.  
Wind changes.  
Pins get tucked behind slopes, bunkers, and collection areas.

Aiming at the middle of the green does three useful things:

1. It widens your effective target.
2. It reduces short-sided misses.
3. It leaves easier chips and putts when you do miss.

That does not mean never attack. It means defaulting to the safer scoring play unless there is a real reason to take on more risk.

## Short-sided misses are score killers

One of the most expensive mistakes in golf is missing on the wrong side of the hole.

When you are short-sided, the next shot gets harder fast:

- Less green to work with
- More spin required
- Higher chance of leaving a difficult putt or another chip

A lot of double bogeys do not come from bad swings. They come from leaving yourself a recovery shot that a better target would have avoided.

Before you commit to a line, ask a simple question:

**If I miss this shot, where does the ball most likely end up?**

If the answer is short-sided, in a bunker, or over a trouble slope, the target is probably too aggressive.

## Choose the target that makes the next shot easier

Good course management is not conservative golf. It is efficient golf.

Think in two-shot sequences:

- Where should this tee shot leave me?
- What angle gives me the best chance to hit the green?
- What target leaves an easier up-and-down if I miss?

On approach shots, the best target is often not the one closest to the hole. It is the one that gives you the highest chance of a two-putt or better.

That might mean:

- Aiming at the center of the green instead of the pin
- Favoring the wide side of the fairway
- Taking one more club to avoid a half-wedge from a bad lie
- Aiming away from the only trouble that can turn a bogey into a double

## A simple pre-shot scoring checklist

Use this before committing to a target:

1. **What is the real trouble here?** Not every hazard is equally relevant.
2. **What miss is most likely for me today?** Aim for your pattern, not a perfect strike.
3. **Where is the easy miss?** Favor the side that leaves a simple next shot.
4. **What score am I protecting?** Sometimes par is a great outcome.
5. **Would the middle of the green still leave a makeable putt?** Usually yes.

This takes ten seconds. It can save two or three shots a round without changing your swing at all.

## Better targets beat better thoughts

Most golfers do not need five new swing thoughts. They need one better decision per hole.

You do not need to rebuild your motion to start scoring better. You need to stop giving away shots with aggressive targets, short-sided misses, and pin hunting on holes that do not require it.

The easiest way to lower your score is often not hitting it farther.

It is aiming where a miss is still playable.

---

**Morning Tee takeaway:** Pick the target that protects your score first. Aggressive lines are fine when the reward is real, but the default play for most golfers should be center-green, wide-fairway, and leave the easy miss.
